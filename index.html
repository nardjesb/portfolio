Research project

Master's research — Graduated with Très Bien

Curcumin-Loaded MgAl Layered Double Hydroxide as a Nano-Therapy for Inflammatory Bowel Disease

USTHB · 2022 · Inter-laboratory collaboration (Cellular Pharmacology & Natural Gases, USTHB)

Chronic inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD) — Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis — affect an estimated 396 cases per 100,000 people worldwide and are conventionally treated with steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, which carry significant side effects. This project developed and evaluated a nanoparticulate alternative: curcumin, a natural antioxidant and anti-inflammatory compound, intercalated into a MgAl layered double hydroxide (LDH) matrix (MgAl-HDL-Cur), synthesized by co-precipitation.

Methods

  • Synthesis by co-precipitation at constant basic pH (pH = 11)
  • Structural characterization by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and FTIR spectroscopy
  • Oxidative-stress biomarkers (MDA, NO) measured under H₂O₂-induced stress
  • Benchmarked against the reference compound 5-fluorouracil (5-FU)

Key finding

XRD confirmed effective curcumin intercalation: the (003) diffraction peak shifted from 2θ = 11.78° to 10.92°, with peak broadening and an increased interlayer parameter, while the hydrotalcite crystal structure remained intact. FTIR showed shifts in the O–H and C=O vibration bands consistent with hydrogen bonding between curcumin and the LDH framework. Biochemically, MDA levels dropped by ~55% in H₂O₂-Cur samples versus H₂O₂ alone (p < 0.0001), indicating a significant reduction in oxidative stress.

Effect of curcumin on MDA levels (oxidative stress marker)

**** p < 0.0001 vs. H₂O₂; ns = not significant. Curcumin (Cur) co-treatment significantly lowered MDA levels in the oxidative-stress (H₂O₂) group, indicating reduced lipid peroxidation.

Challenges & how they were addressed

  • Unavailable animal model. The protocol was originally designed for in vivo validation in a murine model of intestinal inflammation, but mice were not available for the study. The protocol was adapted to an ex vivo approach using human blood samples (red and white blood cell pellets) as the biological substrate for the MDA and NO assays, allowing the antioxidant/anti-inflammatory effect of the nanohybrid to still be evaluated meaningfully.
  • Imperfectly filtered blood samples. The available samples were poorly filtered, containing predominantly red blood cells with only a small fraction of white blood cells, which could limit assay precision. Analysis focused on relative comparisons between treatment groups rather than absolute values, using well-established colorimetric assays (TBARS for MDA, Griess reaction for NO) suited to this type of sample.
  • Curcumin's pH instability. Curcumin degrades rapidly in basic conditions (pH > 8) — exactly the pH range required for HDL co-precipitation. Synthesis pH and reflux conditions were carefully controlled and monitored throughout the co-precipitation step to intercalate curcumin while minimizing its degradation.

Conclusion: Physicochemical characterization confirmed that curcumin was successfully intercalated into the MgAl-HDL framework without altering its specific properties, and biochemical assays showed a significant reduction in oxidative stress markers. This nano-therapy approach appears promising for improving the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profile of curcumin in the treatment of chronic IBD, warranting further in vivo validation as future work.
